Nothing leaves your phone. Every expense, income entry, account name, vehicle, budget and setting is stored in this browser's storage on this device. There is no account, no sign-in, no server and no analytics. The app makes no network request while you use it; after the first visit it opens offline.

We cannot see your data. The people who publish this app have no copy of it and no way to read it. If you lose the phone or clear the site's data, the only copies are the backups you made.

Backups are yours. Copy backup, Save to Files, Download and the PDF report produce files on your phone or clipboard. Where you send them is up to you. Backup files are plain, readable JSON even when a PIN is set.

The PIN encrypts your ledger. With a PIN set, the data in storage is AES-256 encrypted with a random key that only your PIN can unwrap. The PIN is never stored. A forgotten PIN cannot be recovered; the only way back is to erase and restore a backup.

Fingerprint or face unlock adds a passkey on your phone whose secret can also unwrap that key. The passkey lives in your phone's passkey store (iCloud Keychain on iPhone, Google Password Manager on Android), which may sync the key material, never your ledger, between your devices. The PIN remains the fallback. Turning it off removes the passkey's access.

Budget alerts are notifications this app posts on this phone when a save crosses a budget line. Nothing is sent anywhere to produce them; there is no push server.

Error log. The app keeps the last few errors it hit on this phone so that Settings → Report a problem can show them. The log holds no expenses or names, and it is sent only if you copy or email it yourself.

What the site itself can see. Loading the page is an ordinary web request to the host it is served from (currently a static host), which records the request like any website would. The bank and credit-card directory is built into the page; choosing one is not sent anywhere.

Phone storage rules. On iPhone, a Safari tab and the Home Screen app keep separate data, and iOS can clear a Safari tab's data after 7 days unused. On Android, Chrome and the installed app share one store. Either way, install it to the Home Screen and keep a backup.

Changes. If any of this changes, the version number in Settings changes with it and the notes for that version say so.
